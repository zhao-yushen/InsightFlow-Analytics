# Case Study: Revenue Growth with Profit Deterioration

## Business question

A retail management team sees stable or improving revenue, but cash generation appears weaker. Which economic drivers explain the gap, and which action has the best risk-adjusted outcome?

## Data and trust boundary

The included demo profile uses reproducibly simulated transactions, cost and inventory. The case demonstrates analysis methodology and software behaviour; it does not claim a real company's financial result.

## Analysis path

1. Reconcile gross revenue, discount, net revenue, cost of goods and contribution profit.
2. Compare an equal-length current and prior period.
3. Decompose revenue into active customers, purchase frequency and average order value.
4. Bridge contribution-profit change into net-revenue, gross-margin and variable-cost effects.
5. Identify high-revenue low-margin products and markets.
6. Compare pricing, discount, cost and logistics scenarios.
7. Run Monte Carlo simulation over elasticity, demand and cost uncertainty.
8. Convert the highest-priority findings into an owner-based action register.

## Decision principle

A scenario is not recommended merely because expected revenue rises. InsightFlow also tests contribution profit, probability of improvement, downside interval and the probability that revenue increases while profit decreases.

## Experiment lesson

The simulated blanket-discount experiment intentionally increases conversion and revenue while reducing contribution profit. The experiment centre therefore returns “do not roll out,” demonstrating the value of a financial guardrail.

## What would be required for a real decision

- ERP purchase costs and rebates;
- actual logistics and payment fees;
- campaign-level marketing spend;
- returns and fulfilment events;
- approved budget targets;
- experimentally estimated price and promotion response;
- business-owner sign-off on metric definitions.
