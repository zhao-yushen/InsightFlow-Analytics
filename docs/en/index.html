InsightFlow 2.0

A retail analytics and decision platform. Import a sales ledger and it draws where the money comes from and where it leaks away as a single flow, explains why profit changed, points out the issues worth acting on, and simulates the likely result before you change a price, run a promotion or adjust marketing.

  • 17 analysis pages
  • 250 automated tests
  • Runs offline with no external libraries behind the animated components

How it works

How a sales ledger becomes a decision

Every page reads the same ledger of signed amounts. A return is a negative entry, so totals at every level already net out returns. Each line also records where its data came from, and a metric is only as trustworthy as its weakest input.

1 IngestCSV, Excel, Parquet and JSON, including GBK encoding and Chinese headers. You confirm how the columns map.
2 ValidateRaw values are checked before anything is estimated. Failing lines are set aside with the reason recorded.
3 CleanCountries, dates and amounts are standardised, and postage, fees and other non-merchandise lines are picked out.
4 WarehouseA SQLite ledger, dimension tables and a metric catalogue, with a source label on every line.
5 AnalyseProfit bridge, price elasticity, forecasts, churn, inventory and market baskets.
6 DecideScenario simulation, plan search, A/B verdicts and an action list.
7 Deliver17 pages, a question assistant, and reports in HTML, Word and Markdown.

Executive Overview

Where the money went, at a glance

The overview opens with one sentence on what happened this period, such as "Net revenue is up £290.6K on the previous period. The biggest profit lift came from volume; the biggest drag was discounting." The charts below give the evidence for that sentence.

The money river: the income statement as a flow

The river starts with gross sales. As it flows right, discounts, refunds, product cost, shipping, payment, marketing and return handling branch off in turn. Each stream is as wide as its amount, and what remains at the end is contribution profit.

  • Click any outflow and its breakdown by category and by market opens below, along with how its share of sales has moved against the comparison period.
  • The metric strip shows net revenue, contribution profit, margin, orders, customers and return rate, each with its change against the comparison period and a 12-month trend line.
  • You can compare with the previous period or with the same period last year. Retail is seasonal, and a December compared with the quarter before it often makes the peak season look like growth.

Trends, signals and outlook

The trend chart shows two years of net revenue, gross profit and contribution profit. The current, unfinished month is drawn as a dashed line, because a month with too few days in it always looks like a decline.

  • Issues worth acting on are ranked from P0 to P3, for example pressure on profit, a rising return rate or a restocking risk, and each comes with its evidence.
  • Contribution profit for the next three months is shown with 80% and 95% intervals. A wider band means more uncertainty.
  • Progress against each target is listed. Default targets are the average of the last three months plus a rule-based uplift, not an approved budget, and the page says so.

Profitability & Unit Economics

Why profit changed, down to the penny

A price, volume, mix and cost bridge

A change in profit is easy to put down to "sales were good" or "costs went up". The bridge splits it into parts that do not overlap: volume, product mix, list price, discounting, unit cost, new products, discontinued products, shipping, payment fees, marketing and returns.

  • The parts add up to the actual change. The unexplained remainder is shown under the chart, and on the demo data it is £0.00.
  • Mix is an item of its own. If the extra sales come from low-margin products, profit can fall while total volume rises.
  • A second waterfall answers "where did the money go", stepping down from gross sales to contribution profit.

Global Markets

Which markets make money

A globe you can drag

Each market is a bubble sized by net revenue. Teal means its margin is above the overall margin and coral means it is below, so a large market that earns little is easy to spot.

  • Drag to turn the globe, and click a bubble to open that market's details: size, margin, return rate and its main categories.
  • It works from the keyboard too. The left and right arrows turn the globe, the up and down arrows move between markets, and Enter opens the details.
  • It recognises 132 countries and regions, including spellings common in exported files such as EIRE, RSA and USA, as well as Chinese names and ISO codes.

Sales Diagnostics: where the change came from

The monthly trend switches between net revenue, orders, active customers and average order value. The sales mix splits revenue by country, category and channel and then lists the dimensions that moved most against the comparison period. A day-of-week heatmap shows the rhythm of sales.

Customer Value & Churn

Who is worth most, and who may not come back

A model is used only after it passes validation

Customers are first grouped by recency, frequency and spend into Champions, Loyal, Developing, Potential, At Risk and Hibernating. The churn model then predicts whether each customer will buy again in the next 90 days.

  • Validation is out of time. The model is trained on older data and scored on what customers actually did in the following 90 days. If its AUC is below 0.60, the page says so and switches to an interpretable rule-based score.
  • The calibration chart shows whether predicted probabilities match actual repurchase rates. The closer the points sit to the diagonal, the more literally you can read the probabilities.
  • The priority contact list is sorted by expected profit × churn risk and suggests a win-back offer, automated outreach or a cross-sell.
  • Coverage is stated. Anonymous orders are outside the customer analysis, and the page shows what share of revenue carries a customer ID.

Products & Inventory

What sells, what sells together, what to restock

Concentration and baskets

The cumulative revenue curve shows how much revenue depends on the top products, counted over every product rather than the top few hundred. The product efficiency map puts sales next to contribution margin and marks the products that sell a lot but earn little.

  • Products bought together are measured by lift. A lift of 2 means two products appear in the same order twice as often as random pairing would predict.
  • A pair is listed only when it has at least 8 shared orders, so chance is not mistaken for a pattern.

Restocking set by service level

Products fall into nine classes by value (ABC) and demand stability (XYZ). AX items suit automatic replenishment; AZ items need someone watching them. Safety stock depends on the target service level, demand variability and lead-time variability together.

  • Demand is counted net, so an order placed and cancelled on the same day is not demand.
  • The restocking list shows stock on hand, the reorder point, a suggested order quantity, the expected stockout date and the profit a stockout would cost. It can be downloaded.
  • When the data has no stock column, the page analyses demand only and does not invent stock levels to raise restocking alerts.

Pricing Lab

How sensitive customers are to price, and whether to change it

Price elasticity from your own orders

Each category gets a Poisson regression of weekly orders on the price actually paid. The model holds fixed each product's own popularity and each week's seasonal effect, so peak seasons and best sellers are not mistaken for price effects.

  • Each sales channel is tracked on its own. The same product often has different price lists in the web shop and the wholesale channel, and mixing them would read a shift between channels as a price change.
  • Standard errors are clustered by product, and estimates are shrunk toward a category prior. Categories with little data stay close to the industry assumption, those with plenty are driven mainly by the data, and every row says which it is.
  • Every price recommendation shows the profit change at both ends of the 95% elasticity interval, and it counts as robust only if the unfavourable end still makes money.

Historical prices were not set at random, and the estimates hold only near the prices already seen. The page recommends confirming a price change with an A/B test before rolling it out everywhere.

Probabilistic Decision Lab

See thousands of possible results before you decide

Each plan produces a distribution of outcomes

Change price, discount, marketing, purchase cost or return rate, and the lab draws 5,000 possible futures. Price elasticity, marketing response, baseline demand, purchase cost and shipping cost all vary across the draws, and the main uncertainty settings are inputs on the page.

  • Each falling dot is one possible outcome. Red on the left is a fall in profit and teal on the right is an improvement.
  • The lab also reports the median profit change, a bad case (5th percentile) and a good case (95th percentile), the probability of an improvement and the probability of more revenue with less profit.
  • The sensitivity bars show which assumption moves the result most, which is the one worth pinning down first.

A/B Experiment Analysis

Does this experiment support a conclusion?

When the evidence falls short, the verdict is "inconclusive"

An experiment is judged on revenue, profit and returns as well as conversion. Every metric gets a 95% interval, and the evidence leads to one of four verdicts: ship, pilot, inconclusive or stop.

  • Ship requires the whole profit interval to sit above zero. Stop means profit clearly fell or returns crossed a guardrail.
  • A split check raises an alert when the control and treatment groups differ in size more than chance allows, which usually means the assignment went wrong.
  • The page gives the minimum detectable lift and the sample size needed, so you can tell whether the experiment could have found a meaningful effect at all.
  • You can import customer-level results from a real experiment. The demo experiments appear only in the simulated warehouse.

Diagnostics, forecasting and actions

From finding a problem to giving it an owner

Root-cause diagnostics and forecasting

A Shapley decomposition splits the revenue change exactly into active customers, purchase frequency and average order value. The page then finds where the change was largest by country, category and channel, and marks unusual months in the history.

The forecasting page compares up to ten time-series models and one ensemble. It ranks them by rolling-backtest error, picks the most accurate and states how much better it is than repeating last month.

Action Center, assistant and reports

  • The Action Center turns diagnosed issues into actions you can assign, each with an owner, a due date and a status. An issue keeps one record across different date ranges.
  • The assistant takes questions in plain language, such as "Why did profit fall?". It answers only the 12 kinds of question its reviewed queries cover and shows the calculation behind each answer. When it cannot answer, it says so.
  • Business reports come out in one click as HTML, Word and Markdown, with the same numbers as the pages.

Data import

Import your own data; a few bad lines will not sink the file

Below is the import of the public UCI Online Retail II dataset: a 45 MB Excel file with two yearly sheets and 1.06 million lines.

Check, confirm, then import

  • Columns are matched automatically. Headers such as Invoice, StockCode and Price map to order number, product code and unit price, and the page asks you to confirm any match it is unsure of.
  • The pre-import check lists 5,959 problem lines (0.58%, almost all zero prices) and 5,827 non-merchandise lines such as postage and fees, each with its reason and amount. You can download them to check.
  • You decide. "Import data" becomes clickable only after you tick "Set these rows aside and continue". Set-aside lines stay in the warehouse for reference but count toward no metric.
  • By default the import replaces the demo data, so real and simulated data do not mix. The warehouse is backed up before each import, the latest three backups are kept, and you can restore any of them.

The whole file is refused only for a structural problem, such as a missing exchange rate, or when more than 30% of lines fail, which usually means a column was mapped wrongly.

Trust Center

Every number can say where it came from

Estimates are never passed off as audited figures

Each line records a separate source status for the transaction, discount, cost and stock: verified, estimated, simulated or not provided. A metric's trust level depends on its weakest input, weighted by line count.

  • Missing data is not made up. Without a discount column, the paid price is used and revenue reconciles with the source to the penny. Missing stock stays empty, and without categories, products are grouped into families by name.
  • The quality dimensions are completeness, uniqueness, validity, consistency, coverage and contract conformance.
  • Lines left out of the analysis are listed by reason and can be downloaded. Data lineage, import history and the fault drill are on this page too.

Methods and limits

What it does well, and where to be careful

What you can rely on

  • Net revenue, returns and the profit bridge come from the ledger of signed amounts and reconcile to the penny.
  • Every metric states its source and trust level.
  • The forecasting and churn models are used only after a backtest or out-of-time validation.

What needs checking

  • Price elasticity comes from observational data, not a randomised experiment. On the demo data the estimates lean toward zero, and recommendations to raise prices may overstate the gain.
  • Without cost data, profit is estimated by rule and shows the direction only.
  • Default business targets are generated by rules and are not an approved budget.

What a deployment still needs

  • The access code is one shared gate. A company deployment also needs user accounts and permission management.
  • Local backups do not replace off-site backups.
  • The six languages other than Chinese and English translate navigation only.

The methodology sets out each model's approach, assumptions and limits.

Technology and engineering

How it is built

Stack

Python, pandas, NumPy and SQLite handle the data and models; Streamlit and Plotly handle the interface. The money river, globe, metric strip and simulation distribution are drawn by hand in Canvas and SVG, without external libraries, so the app can run fully offline.

Quality

250 automated tests cover rendering and the main click paths on all 17 pages, with 93% coverage. CI runs the tests, Ruff, Bandit and pip-audit on Python 3.11 and 3.13.

Accessibility

Every chart can be switched to a table. Animations follow the system's reduced-motion setting, the money river and globe work from the keyboard, and the palette was checked so that colour-blind viewers can tell the colours apart.

Get started

Run it on your own computer

InsightFlow needs Python 3.10 or later. On Windows you can also double-click run_windows.bat. The first launch generates demo data, which takes about 15 seconds, and the default address is http://127.0.0.1:8501. To switch the interface to English, pick it under "语言 / Language" in the sidebar.

git clone https://github.com/zhao-yushen/InsightFlow-Analytics.git
cd InsightFlow-Analytics
python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -e .
insightflow run
